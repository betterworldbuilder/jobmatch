POST

A salary range in the job post used to be a perk. In more and more US states, it is the rule.

Connecticut's updated pay transparency law took effect October 1, and job ads there now need a wage or wage range.

I am not a lawyer, so check your own situation. But one objection comes up whenever pay ranges do: "If we post a range, we lose negotiating room."

I think it is the other way around.

A real range, even a wide one, does one job well: it removes the offer that was never going to work. The candidate who needed more is gone before interview round one. So is the awkward call in week four.

Start wide. Tighten once you see who is interested.

That is why we built JobsMatchNow around salary up front. In the demo, you see the range and the reasons for the fit before you decide, and a conversation opens only when both sides say yes. Nothing auto-rejects, and you make every call.

If you hire engineers, how do you handle the range today: posted from day one, or shared late?
